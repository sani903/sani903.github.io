Research

I’m interested in what happens when an agent has to act before it has the full picture. Across software-engineering and tool-use settings, my work studies how models recognize missing or unreliable information, how interaction helps them recover what they need, and how to evaluate those behaviors beyond final task success.

Specification adequacy and grounding

I have studied both whether an agent recognizes that its instructions are incomplete and whether its eventual actions are actually supported by the information it was given. Ambig-SWE focuses on underspecified software tasks and the role of clarification, while Latent Critic examines grounding at action time using internal model representations.

Ambig-SWE

Separates task completion from an agent's ability to notice missing task information, ask targeted questions, and use the resulting context.

Latent Critic

Interaction and context

Another part of my work looks at how agents obtain and preserve useful context during execution. Rather than treating more context as automatically better, these projects study which information is worth asking for, how agents explore large environments, and what needs to remain available over a long trajectory.

Asking What Matters

Studies clarification through two practical criteria: whether missing information matters for task success and whether a user can realistically provide it.

Evaluation beyond task completion

Task success alone can hide important differences in how an agent behaves. I have also worked on evaluations that look beyond completion rates: whether agents remain safe in open-ended interactions, and whether the tasks used to develop agents reflect the structure and diversity of real-world work.

OpenAgentSafety

Evaluates agents across multi-turn, multi-user scenarios involving browsers, code execution, messaging, files, and shells over eight safety categories.

Agent development and real-world work

Maps agent benchmarks to occupations and work activities to characterize which parts of real-world work current agent evaluation covers—and which it does not.

Current work

At Samsung Research America, I work on extending the capabilities of small language models for increasingly complex agentic workflows, especially under on-device resource constraints. My public work in this setting includes context-efficient agent execution and specification-grounded tool use.
