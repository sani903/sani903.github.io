Research

My long-term research goal is to understand the capabilities underlying intelligent behavior and develop learning objectives that help these capabilities generalize beyond the tasks on which they are learned. My current work primarily investigates informational reasoning: how agents represent what they know, identify what is missing, and manage information throughout a task.

Modeling task informational state

Agents often proceed despite incomplete instructions or insufficient evidence, even when more information could improve their decisions. I study whether models recognize these gaps and whether their internal representations distinguish grounded from unsupported actions. I am interested in developing explicit, updatable representations of informational state that can guide reliable behavior across tasks.

Separates task completion from an agent's ability to notice missing task information, ask targeted questions, and use the resulting context.

Strategic information acquisition and management

Recognizing an informational gap is only useful if an agent can resolve it. My work studies both how agents seek useful information and how information sources make it available. I am particularly interested in learning strategies that account for the utility and cost of information rather than simply maximizing available context.

Learned parametric memory management

Explores how language models can selectively retain, reinforce, revise, or forget user information in their parameters, combining learned memory policies with mechanisms for controlled persistence and decay.

Evaluation beyond task completion

Task success alone can hide important differences in how an agent behaves or which capabilities it actually possesses. I have worked on evaluations that examine whether agents remain safe in open-ended interactions and whether agent benchmarks reflect the structure and diversity of real-world work. I am interested in extending this perspective to measuring underlying capabilities and their transfer across settings.

OpenAgentSafety

Evaluates agents across multi-turn, multi-user scenarios involving browsers, code execution, messaging, files, and shells over eight safety categories.

Current work

At Samsung Research America, I work on improving the capabilities and reliability of small language models for increasingly complex agentic workflows, especially under on-device resource constraints. My research includes specification-grounded tool use, context-efficient execution, and learned parametric memory management.
